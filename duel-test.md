# Duel test — Cat Reviews Its Human

Proxy judging under the kit brief verbatim (stand-up comic and lifelong
cat servant; criteria in order: (1) funny one-star review written by a
house cat about its human owner; (2) complaint specific and
recognisable — food bowl, closed door, laptop keyboard — rather than
vague grumpiness; (3) voice unmistakably feline: the human measured by
cat standards, the review gives away more about the cat than the owner;
(4) wording tight and surprising, with a turn or a sting in the last
few words that rewards a second read; (5) deadpan shape of a real
one-star review while the content goes somewhere absurd. A pun is
welcome when it serves the complaint; pun stacking does not count).
Each pairing judged both orders; game = mean of the two calls, margin
1–5, winner gets 0.5 + margin/10 per call.

## Candidates (all ≤200 chars)

- C1 "One star. The food bowl was empty for four whole minutes, and he just stood there holding the bag like a hostage negotiator." (124)
- C2 "One star. He closed the door I was sitting in front of, then acted surprised when I screamed through it for an hour." (116)
- C3 "One star. I sat on his laptop to help and he moved me, as if the keyboard was his. It is not. It is warm." (105)
- C4 "One star. He buys the cheap food, serves it late, then calls me picky. I have eaten the same meal for six years. I am not picky. I am correct." (142)
- C5 "One star. He left the house for eight hours and came back smelling of another cat. I have filed this under betrayal and also dinner." (132)
- C6 "One star. The door was open, I walked out, he closed it. The door was closed, I walked in, he opened it. I am the only one working here." (136)
- C7 "One star. He woke me up to show me a video of a cat. I am a cat. I was right here." (82)
- C8 "One star. He put a bell on my collar to track me, then lost me in his own house. The bell is not the problem. He is." (116)
- C9 "One star. I brought him a mouse, a gift, and he screamed. Ungrateful. I will bring him a smaller one next time, so he can learn." (128) ★
- C10 "One star. He moved my sunbeam by opening a curtain the wrong way, then sat in it himself. I have never seen such theft in daylight." (131)
- C11 "One star. He fills the bowl, watches me eat, then says 'good boy' to a girl. The food was fine. The management is confused." (123)
- C12 "One star. He bought a bed for me and slept in his own. I sleep on his pillow now, out of principle and also comfort." (116)
- C13 "One star. The vet visit was described as 'a quick trip'. It was neither quick nor a trip. I have not forgiven the carrier." (122)
- C14 "One star. He talks to me in a baby voice, then ignores me in a grown-up one. I have decided he is two people, and I like neither." (129)
- C15 "One star. I knocked the glass off the table to test gravity. It worked. He is still upset, which tells you everything about him." (128)
- C16 "One star. He locked the cat flap, then asked why I was crying at the window. I was crying at the window because he locked the cat flap." (135)

## First pass

C2, C6, C16 cut (door/flap logic is one joke told three ways; C2's
scream and C16's circular complaint have the weakest final sting);
C4 cut (wordy, the "I am correct" turn lands after the laugh is spent);
C7 cut (short and punchy but the complaint — being woken up — is the
least specific of the set); C8, C10, C11, C12, C13, C14 cut as thinner
on first-read laugh or turn vs the shortlist. Shortlist: C1, C3, C5,
C9, C15.

## Round robin (both orders)

- C9 beats C1 0.70, C3 0.60, C5 0.60, C15 0.60 → **mean 0.625**
- C15 beats C1/C3/C5 0.60 each, loses to C9 0.40 → mean 0.55
- C5 beats C1 0.70, C3 0.60, loses to C9/C15 0.40 → mean 0.525
- C1 beats C3 0.60, loses to C5 0.30, C9 0.30, C15 0.40 → mean 0.40
- C3 loses all four (0.40 each) → mean 0.40

Only C9 clears the 0.60 bar. Decisive pair: C9 vs C15 went to C9 on
criterion #3/#4 — C15's gravity test is a good deadpan joke, but C9's
sting ("a smaller one next time, so he can learn") reveals the cat's
whole gift economy and pedagogy in the last words, margin 1 both
orders. C9 vs C5 went to C9 on #4 as well: C5's "betrayal and also
dinner" is a double sting, but C9's escalation (smaller mouse, to
teach) is the more surprising turn.

## Winner

**C9 — "One star. I brought him a mouse, a gift, and he screamed. Ungrateful. I will bring him a smaller one next time, so he can learn." (128 chars)**

Deadpan one-star shape ("One star."), specific and recognisable
complaint (the gifted mouse, the scream), unmistakably feline voice
(the human is judged by cat standards — ingratitude for a hunted
gift), and the sting lands in the last few words: the cat will not
apologise, it will escalate, smaller, so he can learn.
